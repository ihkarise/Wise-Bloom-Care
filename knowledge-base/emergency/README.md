# Knowledge Base — Emergency Warnings (Curated)

> **This is the ONLY source of emergency-warning content in the product.** Emergency warnings are a carefully curated, reviewed set. The app never generates emergency content or infers an emergency from a user's own numbers (`docs/02-Research/28-MEDICAL_DISCLAIMER.md` BR-4). Emergency styling is reserved (brand BR-1).

## Purpose
Holds the curated red-flag / emergency-warning content surfaced (visually distinct, action-first) to direct users to seek urgent care. Used across pregnancy, postpartum, newborn, and child contexts.

## Principle
The product does not decide whether a situation is an emergency (NG-3). It surfaces pre-authored, sourced emergency warnings that say **seek urgent care now** and direct users to their clinician or local emergency services.

## Examples of red-flag topics (educational framing; confirm wording clinically before ship)
- Pregnancy: severe headache with vision changes; heavy vaginal bleeding; severe abdominal pain; sudden severe swelling; reduced fetal movements (later pregnancy). `[VERIFY wording: ACOG/WHO]`
- Postpartum: heavy bleeding; signs of infection; thoughts of self-harm (route to immediate help/crisis resources). `[VERIFY wording]`
- Newborn/infant: difficulty breathing; poor feeding with lethargy; high fever in a young infant. `[VERIFY wording: AAP/WHO]`

> Each emergency item states the **action first** ("Seek urgent medical care now if…"), is typed `emergency_warning`, is sourced, and is clinically reviewed. Localised emergency numbers/wording per jurisdiction (`docs/02-Research/28` §8).

## Governance
- Curated + clinically reviewed; never auto-generated; never triggered by inference from user values.
- Reserved styling; not reused decoratively.
- Localised per jurisdiction (emergency numbers, wording).

## Sources
WHO; ACOG (preeclampsia warning signs); AAP (infant red flags, on expansion) — see `docs/02-Research/27-REFERENCES.md`. Wording to be finalised with clinical review.
