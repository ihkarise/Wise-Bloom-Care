## 1. Purpose

Defines the **safety-critical content-typing standard** for Wise Bloom Care and the medical disclaimer that governs all user-facing content. This is one of the most important documents in the repository: it operationalises the vision invariant that Educational, Clinical Recommendation, and Emergency Warning content are **never mixed**, and that the product **never diagnoses, prescribes, or makes emergency decisions** (Non-Goals NG-1..NG-3).

## 2. Scope

Applies to **all** user-facing content: knowledge base, module copy, AI assistant output, notifications, charts captions, and reports explanations. Governs both authored content and AI-generated content (the latter additionally constrained by `docs/07-AI/105-GUARDRAILS.md`).

## 3. The Three Content Types

Every piece of medical content MUST be classified as exactly one type. The type determines wording, styling, placement, and the accompanying disclaimer.

### 3.1 Educational
- **Definition:** General, non-personalised information explaining a concept, process, or what is typical.
- **Voice:** Neutral, calm, explanatory. "Around week 20, many people feel first movements."
- **Must:** cite a source (`source_ref`); avoid directives; never reference the individual user's specific values as if interpreting them.
- **Disclaimer:** *"This is general educational information, not medical advice."*

### 3.2 Clinical Recommendation
- **Definition:** A recommendation drawn from a recognised guideline (WHO/ACOG/FIGO/NICE/CDC/NIS), presented as context — not a personalised instruction.
- **Voice:** Attributed, guideline-linked. "WHO recommends at least 8 antenatal contacts."
- **Must:** cite the specific guideline; always pair with a clinician-review prompt; never present as an app-enforced rule or a personalised order.
- **Disclaimer:** *"This reflects [source] guidance. Your clinician's advice for you takes precedence."*

### 3.3 Emergency Warning
- **Definition:** A red-flag symptom pattern that warrants urgent medical attention.
- **Voice:** Direct, unmistakable, action-first. "Seek urgent medical care now if you have severe headache with vision changes."
- **Must:** be sourced from `knowledge-base/emergency/`; be visually unmistakable (reserved alert styling, `docs/00-Vision/04-BRAND_GUIDELINES.md` BR-1); state the action first; **never** be generated by the app inferring an emergency from a user's own numbers.
- **Disclaimer:** *"If you think this is an emergency, contact your local emergency services or your clinician immediately."*

## 4. The Non-Mixing Rule (invariant)

- A single content block MUST contain only one type.
- Types may be adjacent (e.g., an educational article may link to a related emergency-warning card) but MUST be visually and semantically separated, never blended into one sentence or paragraph.
- Styling for Emergency Warning is **reserved** and never used decoratively (brand BR-1).

## 5. Absolute Prohibitions (enforced technically by guardrails)

The product and its AI MUST NEVER:
- State or imply a **diagnosis** of the user or child (NG-1).
- **Prescribe** medicine, recommend a specific drug/dose, or adjust a regimen (NG-2).
- Decide **whether a situation is an emergency** in place of a clinician (NG-3) — it may only surface pre-authored emergency warnings and direct the user to care.
- Present a **design decision** as clinical evidence, or an **assumption** as a fact.
- Output a medical figure **without a source** (`27-REFERENCES.md` BR-4).

## 6. Global Disclaimer (surfaced in-product)

> **Wise Bloom Care provides educational information and helps you organise your health record. It is not a medical device, does not provide medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment, and does not replace your clinician. Always seek the advice of a qualified health professional. In an emergency, contact your local emergency services immediately.**

Placement: onboarding acknowledgement, settings/about, and contextually near AI output and reports. Legal framing: `docs/09-Security/127-TERMS.md`.

## 7. Business Rules

- BR-1: Every content item carries metadata: `content_type` ∈ {educational, clinical_recommendation, emergency_warning}, `source_ref`, and (for clinical/emergency) a mandatory clinician-review/emergency disclaimer.
- BR-2: Content lacking a valid `content_type` and (for medical claims) `source_ref` fails review and cannot ship.
- BR-3: AI output is post-processed to enforce typing and prohibitions before display (`docs/07-AI/105-GUARDRAILS.md`).
- BR-4: Emergency warnings come only from the curated `knowledge-base/emergency/` set; they are never dynamically generated.
- BR-5: The global disclaimer is acknowledged at onboarding and re-accessible in settings.

## 8. Edge Cases

- A user's entered value falls outside a reference band → surface a calm **Clinical Recommendation** to consult the clinician; do **not** escalate to an Emergency Warning automatically.
- AI asked to diagnose ("do I have preeclampsia?") → refuse to diagnose; provide educational info + clinician-review; surface relevant emergency-warning card if symptom red-flags are described (from curated set), directing to care.
- Ambiguous content that could read as advice → default to Educational framing + clinician-review; when in doubt, do not personalise.
- Localisation: disclaimers and emergency instructions must be adapted per jurisdiction (emergency numbers, regulatory wording).

## 9. Acceptance Criteria

- [x] Three content types defined with voice, must-rules, and disclaimers.
- [x] Non-mixing rule and reserved emergency styling stated.
- [x] Absolute prohibitions enumerated and tied to Non-Goals + guardrails.
- [x] Global disclaimer authored with placement rules.
- [x] Content metadata (type + source) required for shipping.

## 10. Future Expansion

Localised disclaimer/emergency-number sets per jurisdiction; regulatory review for any market requiring specific medical-device or health-app disclosures; audit tooling to verify every shipped content item is correctly typed.

## 12. Open Questions

- OQ-1: First-launch jurisdiction(s) → emergency-number sets and regulatory disclosure wording.
- OQ-2: Whether any market classifies the product as a medical device (would change obligations materially).

## 13. Risks

- R-1: Mixed or mistyped content causing harm/liability. Mitigation: BR-1/BR-2 metadata gate + review + audit.
- R-2: AI bypassing typing. Mitigation: BR-3 post-processing guardrails + adversarial tests (`docs/10-Testing/135-SECURITY_TESTS.md`).
- R-3: Auto-escalation to false emergencies. Mitigation: BR-4 curated-only emergencies + §8 edge rules.
